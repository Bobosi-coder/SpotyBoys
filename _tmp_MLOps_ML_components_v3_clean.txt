1 Overview of Changes from v4.0
Version 5.0 replaces the PANNs audio embedding pipeline withItem2Vec. The primary motivation
is engineering feasibility: the PANNs approach requires downloading approximately 4.5 million 30-
second MP3 preview clips from Deezer, a process estimated to take∼10 days of continuous operation
with a hit rate of only∼30%. Item2Vec eliminates all external downloads and trains entirely on
the session interaction data already on disk.
Table 1: Key differences between v4.0 (PANNs) and v5.0 (Item2Vec).
Property v4.0 (PANNs) v5.0 (Item2Vec)
Embedding source Acoustic content (audio waveform) User behaviour (session co-occurrence)
External data required∼4.5M MP3 downloads None
Preprocessing time∼10 days (download) + 4–6h (GPU)∼1 hour (CPU only)
Catalog coverage∼30% of session tracks∼80–90% of session interactions
Embedding dimension 2048→128 (TruncatedSVD) 128 (direct)
Cold-item handling Any track with an MP3 clip Tracks with≥5session appearances
GPU required Yes (PANNs forward pass) No
ALS branch Removed (v4.0 decision) Not added back
Tag features Removed (v4.0 decision) Not added back
Scalar features Removed (v4.0 decision) Not added back
All other architectural decisions from v4.0 — the GRU ranker, the three-branch retriever (co-
occurrence, Pref NN, popularity), the multi-centroid user preference vector, the joint BCE+BPR
training objective, and the policy re-ranker — are carried forward unchanged. The document below
is self-contained; v4.0 need not be consulted.

2.2 Why ALS and Tag Features Are Excluded
ALS.The positive-only interaction matrix spans 44,807 users×3,324,298 tracks at a density of
0.0098%. In this extreme sparsity regime, ALS latent factors for long-tail tracks are numerically
unreliable (the gradient signal is dominated by a tiny fraction of popular items). The full positive-
and-love matrix has essentially the same density (0.0098%). ALS is therefore excluded.
Tagfeatures.70.5%oftracksinthecataloghavenotagannotationswhatsoever. ATF-IDF/SVD
encoding over such a sparse, skewed vocabulary would produce near-zero vectors for the majority
of tracks and is not expected to add useful signal. Tag features are excluded.

risk from this choice.

3.5 Stage 4 (A) — Item2Vec Training Corpus
Purpose.Transformsession_tracks.csvinto a list of ordered track-ID sequences suitable for
Word2Vec training. Each session becomes one sentence; eachtrack_idbecomes one word.
Label filtering rationale.
•Positive(73.9%): completed or substantially completed listening. Strong co-occurrence signal.
•Neutral(7.0%): partial listening but not a skip. Retained as weaker co-occurrence evidence.
•Skip(4.0%): explicit negative feedback. Including skip events would train the model that
skip-pairs are semantically related, which is the opposite of the desired signal. Excluded.
•Unknown(15.1%): playratio is null; no interpretable signal. Excluded.

3.6 Stage 5 (B) — Item2Vec Model Training
Algorithm.Item2Vec treats each session sequence as a sentence and eachtrack_idas a word, then
applies the Word2Vec Skip-gram objective. The Skip-gram model maximises the log-probability of
observing context tracks within a sliding window given a centre track:

Why Skip-gram over CBOW.Skip-gram predicts context items from a centre item; it per-
forms better on rare items because each training example focuses on one item at a time. In our
long-tail catalog, many tracks appear infrequently, making Skip-gram the preferred variant.

3.7 Stage 6 (C) — Embedding Validation
This stage performs automated checks to catch training failures before downstream components are
built.
1.Same-artist similarity check.Sample 200 same-artist track pairs. Verify that their mean
cosine similarity is higher than the mean cosine similarity of 200 random track pairs. Failure
indicates that the model has not learned musically coherent structure.
2.Numerical health check.Assert: no NaN or infinite values initem2vec_128d.npy; embed-
ding mean≈0(Skip-gram produces approximately zero-centred vectors); standard deviation
∈(0.1,2.0).

5 Top-Level Architecture
The system recommends the next 5 tracks during an active listening session on a Navidrome-based
personal music server. The pipeline has four stages:
C1: Track Encoder (Item2Vec)→C2: Multi-Recall Retriever→C3: GRU Ranker

C1 runs entirely offline; its outputs are materialised to disk and frozen before any serving begins.

8 Component 3: GRU Ranker
8.1 Purpose
C3 is the only component withlearned parameters. It scores each of the≤200candidates in
Ct under the current session context and user long-term preference, producing a ranked list from
which C4 selects the final Top-5.
Decision:The session encoder is a 2-layer GRU, not a Transformer. The session median length
is 6 tracks. At sequences of this length, Transformer self-attention provides no meaningful advantage
over GRU recurrence, and the GRU eliminates the need for positional encodings. GRU’s sequential
inductive bias is a natural fit for ordered listening history, where the most recent track exerts the
strongest influence.

8.3 Training
8.3.1 Key Design Decision: Full-Catalog Negative Sampling
Decision:C3 training data is constructed by sampling from the full item2vec catalog, not from
C2’s retrieval output.
If training were restricted to C2’s 200 candidates, C3 could only learn to re-rank what C2 already
retrieved. Tracks outside C2’s recall ceiling would never appear as positives during training, hard-
capping C3’s generalisation. By sampling from the full catalog, C3 learns a general relevance
function that transfers to any set of candidates presented by C2 at serving time.
8.3.2 Training Sample Construction
For each training session of lengthN≥2, iterate over positionst= 1, . . . , N−2:
1.Prefix:[i 1, . . . , it], left-padded to lengthL= 20.

•positive:y= 1.0,ω= 1.0
•neutral:y= 0.5,ω= 0.3

11.3 Retraining Trigger and Promotion
Retraining triggers.
•Scheduled: weekly.
•Triggered: 7-day rolling skip rate increases>3percentage points; fallback rate>10%; or
average play ratio on recommended tracks drops below 0.60.
Two-gate promotion.
1.Offline gate:New model achieves≥95%of the current model’s NDCG@5 on a 1-week holdout.
2.Shadow gate:24-hour shadow scoring; promote if shadow scores correlate with positive out-
comes at least as well as production scores.
Rollback.If the promoted model’s skip rate increases>5percentage points within 48 hours,
auto-revert to the previous checkpoint.

13 Summary of Key Decisions
Table 24: Index of all binding architectural decisions.
ID Decision
D1 Item2Vec (Skip-gram, 128-d) replaces PANNs audio embeddings.
Justification: PANNs requires∼10 days of downloads with 30%
hit rate; Item2Vec trains in∼1 hour on local data.
D2 Item2Vec is trained onall available sessions(not restricted to
the training split), because it is unsupervised and carries no label-
leakage risk.
D3 Item2Vec catalog is defined bymin_count=5: only tracks with≥5
session appearances receive an embedding.
D4 ALS collaborative filtering is excluded. Interaction matrix density
of 0.0098% makes ALS factors unreliable for long-tail tracks.
D5 Tagfeaturesareexcluded. 70.5%oftrackshavenotagannotations.
D6 Scalar metadata features are excluded. The only track representa-
tion is the 128-d Item2Vec embedding.
D7 Three recall branches: co-occurrence (100) + Pref NN (80) + pop-
ularity (20). No ALS branch.
D8 Pref NN FAISS index is built over Item2Vec embeddings (not au-
dio). Pref NN therefore captures co-occurrence-space similarity,
not acoustic similarity.
D9 Multi-centroid K-Means (K≤3) for user long-term preference.
Single mean embedding loses cluster structure for diverse users.
D10u long at serving time is the centroid nearest to the current session’s
mean embedding, not a fixed centroid.
D11 Session encoder is a 2-layer GRU, not a Transformer. Session me-
dian length is 6; GRU provides equivalent quality with simpler
serving code and no positional encoding.
D12 C3 training uses full-catalog negative sampling to avoid hard-
capping on C2’s recall ceiling.
D13 Joint loss: BCE (pointwise, weighted by label) + 0.5×BPR (pair-
wise).
D14 Data split is random session-level (75/12.5/12.5). Timestamps are
unreliable; temporal split is not used.
D15 C4: hard-block replays and dislikes; same-artist soft penalty (β=
0.15); greedy MMR diversity (δ= 0.05);ϵ-greedy exploration (ϵ=
0.10) at position 5.
D16 Retraining is weekly by default, with skip-rate and play-ratio KPI
triggers. Two-gate promotion with auto-rollback.
